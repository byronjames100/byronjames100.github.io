What I Stand On

The Leadership Code

My leadership philosophy wasn't built in a classroom. It was forged through nine years of Army Airborne Infantry service — developing soldiers into high-performing assets who could operate independently, under pressure, and without supervision. What I learned about people, standards, and servant leadership in uniform has never left me.

These are the principles I carry into every team, every business, and every partnership.

01
Servant First

The leader's job is to create conditions for others to be great. Authority is a stewardship, not a reward. I put the mission and the people ahead of my own comfort, ego, or recognition — always.

02
The Multiplier

I can't make most people experts. But I can make a lot of people very good at the fundamentals — and that changes organizations. The light bulb moment in someone's eyes is my fuel. That never gets old.

03
Standards Without Ego

I hold high expectations for myself first, then my team. Practical. Easy going. Professional. I set the bar and I live at it — because you cannot hold others to a standard you don't carry yourself.

04
Simple, Real, Repeatable

I build systems that don't require genius to run — they require consistency. Every team, every process, every culture I build is designed to work without me. That's not a weakness. That's the point.

"I am out of a job when everyone around me has become who they were meant to be as a virtuous leader and professional. That's the goal. That's always been the goal."

Culture

What I Build Around People

The Culture I Create

Culture isn't a perk list or a values poster. It's what happens when the leader isn't in the room. It's the standard that lives in the people — not in the handbook. Here is what teams I lead actually experience.

  • A deep, rich, and unique environment where people know exactly where they stand and why it matters
  • High expectations — held first by me, then by the team, and eventually by each other
  • Practical, grounded leadership that is easy going without ever losing the standard
  • Faith-informed values that create psychological safety and genuine accountability at the same time
  • A team that chooses to do hard things and gets better every time they do
  • A place where professional growth and personal growth happen simultaneously — because they always do

The Development Cycle

1

Find the Raw Material

Identify people who want to accelerate — not just show up. Potential matters more than polish.

2

Translate the Complex

Break high-level skills into digestible, actionable fundamentals. Everyone can be very good at the basics.

3

Watch the Light Come On

The moment of clarity. The shift from "I don't know if I can" to "I've got this." That's the fuel.

4

Send Them Forward

The goal is leaders who don't need me. When they no longer do — the cycle worked. Start again.
